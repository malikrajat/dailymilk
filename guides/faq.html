Milk delivery record keeping: FAQ

Short, direct answers to the questions that come up most often. Each answer stands on its own; the longer guides go deeper where you need it.

By Rajat Malik · 17 November 2026 · 8 min read

How should I record milk delivered in two shifts?

Record the morning and evening rounds as separate entries with the same date. Each entry carries its own quantity, rate and return, so the two shifts can be checked independently. Merging them into one daily total hides which shift a shortage or return belongs to.

What if the milk rate changes in the middle of the month?

Split the billing period at the change date and total each part at the rate that applied. Store the rate on every entry rather than only the current price, so an old bill can be recalculated correctly. Applying the new rate to the whole month overcharges or undercharges every customer.

Worked example of a mid-month rate change

How do I record milk that was returned?

Record the return as its own line, valued at the rate of the delivery it reverses, with a short reason such as sour, damaged, customer away or over-delivered. Never reduce the original delivery quantity: keeping the two separate preserves both the customer's usage and the reason for the loss.

Do I need to track fat and SNF?

Only if your price depends on composition. If you charge a flat rate per litre, fat and SNF are optional quality notes. If your rate is calculated from composition, you must store the fat reading, the SNF reading and the multipliers for every delivery, because a calculated price alone cannot be audited later.

How often should I back up my records?

At least monthly, and weekly if the round is your main income. Keep two copies in two places, export in an open format such as CSV or JSON, and test the restore once. An export that has never been opened is not yet a backup.

Does Daily milk work offline?

Yes. Daily milk is a Progressive Web App, so the screens it has already loaded keep working without a connection. Entries made offline are kept on the device and sync when the connection returns. Install it from the browser menu for the most reliable offline experience.

Where is my data stored in Daily milk?

Business records are stored in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Cloud Storage) under your own account, so they are available on every device you sign in from. Authentication is handled by Firebase Authentication; the app never receives your Google password. You can export the full database as JSON at any time.

Can I export records for my accountant?

Yes. Daily milk produces CSV and PDF reports, and the full database can be exported as a JSON file. CSV opens in any spreadsheet or accounting package. Agree the period and format with your accountant before month end so nothing has to be re-entered.

Why do my litre totals not match my supplier's bill?

The usual causes are a missing entry, a return recorded as a reduced delivery, a split delivery written on one line, or a can measured once and assumed thereafter. Reconcile received minus delivered minus returns for a week before assuming an error on the supplier's side.

What should I do when a customer is away?

Write a zero with a reason and the return date on the route sheet, and agree in advance whether delivery is paused or left. Recording an explicit zero prevents the day from looking like a missing entry, and it stops milk being left out and charged for.

Do these guides replace an accountant or tax adviser?

No. The guides explain practical record keeping for a milk delivery business. They are educational and are not accounting, tax, legal or financial advice. Figures and formats vary by place and by business, so confirm tax and invoicing requirements with a qualified professional.
