Cookies

Cookie policy

This page explains the small amount of storage this site uses itself, the cookies set by Google advertising on the public guides, and how to control them.

Last updated: 17 November 2026

The short version

  • The guides and policy pages set no first-party cookies. They store nothing in your browser except what your browser or an extension does on its own.
  • The public guides load Google AdSense on content pages. Google and its partners may set advertising cookies and read them on later visits.
  • The app stores sign-in and preference data in your browser so it can work offline. That storage is functional, not advertising.
  • You control advertising cookies. Google's consent message appears where the law requires it, and the links below let you change your choice at any time.

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small file a site asks your browser to store. Related technologies — local storage, session storage, IndexedDB and cache storage — work differently but serve a similar purpose: remembering something between page loads or between visits. This policy covers all of them.

Storage used by this site

Public guides, about, contact and policy pages

These pages are static HTML. They load one stylesheet from this site and, on content pages only, the Google AdSense script. They do not set a first-party cookie, do not use analytics, and do not require an account.

The application

The app is a Progressive Web App. It uses browser storage for functional purposes only:

Functional storage used by the app. None of it is used for advertising.
Storage Purpose Lifetime
Firebase Authentication storage Keeps you signed in and restores your session Until you sign out or clear site data
Local storage (theme and preferences) Remembers your colour theme and display choices Until you clear site data
IndexedDB and cache storage Keeps the app usable offline and queues entries made without a connection Until the cache is replaced or cleared

Advertising cookies on the guides

Public content pages load Google AdSense. Google and its advertising partners may set cookies to frequency-cap ads, measure performance, detect invalid traffic and, where you have consented, personalise advertising. Common cookies include:

Common Google advertising cookies. The exact set can change; Google's own documentation is authoritative.
Cookie Set by Purpose
__gads, __gpi Google Ad delivery, frequency capping and measurement
__eoi Google Fraud and invalid-traffic detection
IDE DoubleClick / Google Ad personalisation and reporting, where consented
test_cookie DoubleClick Checks whether the browser accepts cookies

Advertising cookies are set by Google's domains, not by this site, and they may be read on other sites that use Google advertising. Third parties may place and read cookies, web beacons, IP addresses and other identifiers as a result of ad serving on this site. This site does not set cookies on Google's domains, and does not modify, intercept or delete cookies set on Google's domains. For the complete and current list, see Google's types of cookies and how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Consent

Where the law requires consent for advertising cookies — for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and Brazil — Google's certified consent management message is used. It appears before advertising cookies are set for those visitors and records your choice. This supports compliance with the GDPR, the UK GDPR and the Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados (LGPD). You can change or withdraw that choice at any time:

  • Open any guide page and use Cookie settings in the footer. That re-opens Google's consent message where it is available.
  • Use the browser controls described below to delete cookies already stored.

Where consent is not required, Google may still use cookies for advertising, and the controls below let you limit personalisation.

How to control cookies

Google ad personalisation

Turn off personalised advertising across Google services at Google Ad Settings. You will still see ads, but they will not be based on your activity.

Industry opt-out tools

  • Digital Advertising Alliance (US)
  • European Interactive Digital Advertising Alliance
  • Network Advertising Initiative

Browser controls

Every current browser lets you view, delete and block cookies, and most offer a private window that discards them when you close it. Blocking all cookies will not stop the guides from loading, but it will sign you out of the app and disable offline storage. See your browser's help pages for the exact steps.

Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

Browsers can send a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal. This site does not run its own analytics, so there is nothing first-party to switch off. Google's advertising systems handle these signals according to Google's own policies; the Ad Settings link above is the direct control.

Your other rights

The privacy policy explains what personal information is processed, the legal bases relied on, retention, and how to request access, correction or deletion. Advertising-related requests that concern Google's data can also be raised with Google using the links in that policy.

Changes to this policy

If the cookies used by this site change, this page and its "last updated" date change with them. Material changes will also be reflected in the privacy policy.
